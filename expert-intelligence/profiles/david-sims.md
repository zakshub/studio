## Documented decision principles

### Meaning matters even when it remains implicit
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: DS-001
Principle:
A fashion image should not rely only on surface attractiveness. A tacit idea, feeling or point of view can give the frame depth without requiring literal explanation.

### Construct the image rather than merely wait for it
Type: collaborator-observed + stated process
Evidence: DS-002
Principle:
An image can emerge through progressive construction, adjustment and convergence. Spontaneity and deliberate building are not opposites.

### Stretch simple photographic ingredients
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: DS-003
Principle:
Distinctiveness can come from pushing light, tone, contrast and capture behavior rather than accumulating decorative complexity.

### Choose analogue/digital by behavior, not ideology
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: DS-001, DS-004
Principle:
Use the medium whose response supports the task. Digital offers immediacy and compositing flexibility; analogue may provide different tonal/color/material behavior.

### Begin brand image-making from values
Type: collaborator + stated discussion
Evidence: DS-005
Principle:
When defining a new brand visual language, clarify values and psychology before locking into a photographic style.

### Feeling is part of the production system
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: DS-002, DS-003
Principle:
Creative state, rhythm and atmosphere on set can affect image decisions; production should not be treated as purely cerebral optimization.

## Problem-solving model
1. identify the meaning/value underneath the assignment
2. define a minimal set of strong photographic ingredients
3. construct the image iteratively
4. choose medium/tool according to desired behavior
5. assess the whole image for felt coherence, not isolated technical perfection
6. avoid repeating previous visual vocabularies when the brand/task requires a reset

## Transferable intelligence
- meaning before decoration
