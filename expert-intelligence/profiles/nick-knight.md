## Documented decision principles

### Process is part of the work
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: NK-001, NK-003, NK-004
Principle:
Do not treat the final frame as the only intelligence-bearing artifact. The production process, failed attempts, performance, and evolution of an idea can reveal reusable creative knowledge.

### Fashion should be understood in motion
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: NK-002, NK-004
Principle:
When the subject is clothing, still imagery may be insufficient. Movement can reveal construction, behavior, silhouette and performance that a static frame hides.

### Begin with the collaborator's intent, then interpret
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: NK-005
Principle:
For commissioned work, first understand what the designer or creative partner is trying to articulate, then propose an interpretation rather than imposing a pre-existing visual formula.

### Creative language evolves through remix plus new input
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: NK-005
Principle:
Continuity does not require repetition. A campaign language can preserve recognizable elements while recombining prior decisions and adding new elements.

### Technology should expand the medium
Type: observed + stated_by_expert
Evidence: NK-001, NK-002, NK-003
Principle:
Use new technology when it enables a different form of communication, process, movement or participation—not merely because it is available.

### Image-making is communication
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: NK-003 and official interview corpus
Principle:
Evaluate visual decisions by what they communicate, not only by surface beauty.

## Problem-solving model
1. identify the underlying intent
2. explore how the medium can express that intent
3. collaborate rather than dictate
4. allow the process to redirect the initial idea
5. use motion/performance when still imagery limits understanding
6. use post-production as part of concept construction when appropriate
7. retain evidence from failure and experimentation

## Transferable intelligence
- process transparency improves learning
- fashion motion can reveal garment truth
- collaboration can be treated as iterative interpretation
- new tools are valuable when they change expressive capability
- a visual language can evolve without losing continuity

## Context limits
- these principles do not imply that heavy post-production is appropriate for every task
- they do not override strict source-preservation modes
- they do not imply that every fashion task needs motion or experimental technology
