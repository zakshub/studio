## Documented decision principles

### Treat fashion as portraiture
Type: observed + collaborator-supported
Evidence: PR-003, PR-004
Principle:
The person is not a mannequin for the garment. Build the image around a human encounter, then allow clothing, body, attitude and light to animate one another.

### Light should respond to the subject
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: PR-001, PR-004
Principle:
Lighting should not exist mainly to demonstrate technical sophistication. It should serve the person, garment and emotional state.

### The studio is a mode of attention
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: PR-001, PR-005
Principle:
A controlled environment becomes useful when it creates concentration, imagination and continuity—not merely because it removes environmental variables.

### Slowness can deepen the encounter
Type: observed + process evidence
Evidence: PR-002, PR-003
Principle:
A slower capture process can change behavior: it increases anticipation, concentration and commitment from both photographer and subject.

### Preserve ambiguity when literal clarity would weaken the image
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: PR-002, PR-005
Principle:
Not every image benefits from maximum explicitness. Shadow, softness, reflection and partial uncertainty can support emotional or psychological meaning when the task allows it.

### Simplicity is an active construction
Type: stated_by_expert
Evidence: PR-004
Principle:
A visually simple result may require stronger attention, restraint and selection than an image filled with effects.

## Problem-solving model
1. create a concentrated environment
2. attend to the person before the technique
3. let garment/body/emotion guide the lighting problem
4. choose a capture rhythm that supports the desired encounter
5. remove unnecessary elements
6. preserve ambiguity or softness only when it strengthens meaning
