## Visual structure observed

The campaign moves through a dense South Asian market and older commercial streetscape rather than a controlled fashion set. Garments are placed against shutters, brick, timber doors, shop interiors, hanging goods, counters and pedestrian movement.

Repeated visual devices across the sequence include:

1. Subject framed through doors, counters, shelves and foreground objects.
2. Partial obstruction of the model to create depth and an observed rather than staged camera position.
3. Frequent switching between street, threshold, interior stall and shopfront while remaining inside one coherent neighborhood world.
4. Ordinary market actions and browsing behavior mixed with conventional fashion poses.
5. Strong contrast between polished garments and imperfect commercial surfaces.
6. Environmental texture retained rather than cleaned away.
7. Vertical compositions that often use architectural edges and merchandise as natural side frames.

## Promoted learning

Three rules were sufficiently distinct from the current editorial fashion brain to promote:

EF-020 Vernacular commerce as an active set

EF-021 Motivated foreground occlusion

EF-022 Micro-location sequencing inside one world

## Rejected as duplicate

Refined garment against rough environment was not promoted because EF-001 and existing rough versus refined intelligence already cover it.

Ordinary behavior in a heritage setting was not promoted because EF-018 already covers lived heritage direction.

General frame within frame composition was not promoted as a new rule because it already exists elsewhere in the visual production system and is not unique to this dataset.

Vertical social framing was rejected as too generic.
