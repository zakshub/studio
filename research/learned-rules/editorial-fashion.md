# Learned Rules — Editorial Fashion

These rules are promoted from dated research only when they add reusable intelligence beyond the base Production Intelligence and Photographic Reality systems.

## 2026-09-18

### EF-001 Structural-background contrast
For soft, flowing, modest or highly embellished garments, hard architectural materials such as glass, concrete and metal can increase perceived garment softness and premium value through material contrast. Do not assume luxury clothing requires ornate or heritage backgrounds.

### EF-002 Environment-color suppression
When a hero garment needs stronger dominance, first consider lowering/deepening competing environment color families before increasing garment saturation. Relative chroma contrast is often more natural than simply making the garment louder.

### EF-003 Mixed-light continuity
Commercial luxury can blend controlled strobe with soft environmental light. The strobe should increase face/product clarity without creating a separate 'studio-lit subject pasted into ambient scene' look.

### EF-004 Luxury through scale
Sparse monumental environments can create luxury through scale, emptiness and texture instead of decorative props. Preserve garment readability with palette separation and controlled contrast.

### EF-005 Variable-light campaign continuity
A single campaign may use multiple lighting architectures—open shade, backlit window, golden-hour sun, soft portrait light—without losing consistency if styling, palette, material response and subject direction remain coherent.

### EF-006 Motion continuity across locations
When environments change, repeated kinetic cues (walking energy, fabric movement, gaze rhythm, posture family) can serve as the continuity system for the campaign.

### EF-007 Shadow geometry as composition
Physically plausible hard shadows from architecture or sunlight can be deliberately composed as graphic elements. Treat shadow shape as part of layout/hierarchy, not only as lighting fallout.

### EF-008 Material-aware analogue texture
Film grain/analogue character should vary perceptually with material, exposure and depth. Avoid uniform grain overlays that flatten fabric, skin and environment into one texture field.
