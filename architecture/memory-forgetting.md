# Memory, Consolidation, Forgetting and Deprecation

## Memory classes
### Working
Current task state. Short-lived.

### Episodic
What happened in a specific task, practice session, research cycle, approval, failure, or campaign.

### Semantic
General facts and validated reusable principles.

### Procedural
How to perform a workflow or skill.

### Project
Decisions, assets, constraints, and history within one project/collection/campaign.

### Expert
Evidence-backed profiles, methods, observations, and scoped principles associated with specific experts.

### Failure
Known failure patterns, causes, mitigations, and recurrence.

## Consolidation
Scheduled consolidation should:
1. group similar recent episodes
2. identify repeated success/failure patterns
3. detect contradictions
4. identify candidate abstractions
5. compare against canonical knowledge
6. create learning candidates
7. leave universal promotion gated

## Forgetting
Forgetting means reducing retrieval weight or active relevance, not silently destroying evidence.

Triggers:
- duplicate knowledge
- stale platform-specific behavior
- superseded technical detail
- repeatedly disproven hypothesis
- low-value isolated observation
- obsolete executor behavior

## Deprecation
Deprecated knowledge remains historically traceable but is excluded from normal production retrieval.

## Non-forgetting rules
Never automatically forget:
- rights/provenance events
- critical safety/security events
- approval/rejection history
- source lineage
- material failures that affected production correctness
